The last time I built a website, I built it in Microsoft FrontPage. It turned out to be the last time, and I didn't realize it yet.

Then 20 years went by.

When I decided to bring this blog back, I expected to feel rusty. Maybe a little lost. That's not what happened.

## Like I never stopped

I sat down with Claude Code, described what I wanted, and started building. Within the first session, something strange happened: it felt familiar. Not the tools. The tools are completely different. The feeling. The pull of "what if I try this next?" came right back, like it had been waiting the whole time.

Twenty years gone, and it felt like I'd only stepped away for a weekend.

## Then and now

FrontPage was how a lot of us got started, and I'm grateful for it. But I also remember the fiddling. Hunting through menus. Fighting with layouts. Being stuck on something with nobody to ask, besides people like me hanging out on the Experts Exchange community, where often nobody knew how to fix the problem either.

But maybe it was the friends we made along the way. Experts Exchange is where I met Carl Webster from the Citrix community, and where I got my start as an EUC community guy, frantically googling other people's Citrix problems and posting the most likely answers, collecting expert points like arcade tickets. That spirit is still in me today, and it's a big part of why I lead the World of EUC Chicago chapter.

Building this way is different. I describe what I want, see it take shape, and adjust. When I don't know how something works, I ask, and I get an explanation instead of a dead end. The boring parts shrink and the fun part, actually making something, moves to the front.

Building websites is easier now than FrontPage ever made it. So is building apps. So is writing blog posts, including this one. That's not a shortcut. It just means more of my time goes to the parts I enjoy.

It's more fun than building websites ever was for me. And more rewarding, because I'm learning as I go instead of just getting stuck.

## Building in public

Here's the part I want to be upfront about: I don't know how to do a lot of this yet.

I'm going to ask basic questions. I'm going to get things wrong. Some of it will happen right here on this blog, where you can see it. I'm fine with that. Looking a little silly is the price of learning something new, and it's a small price.

If you've been putting off starting something because you're worried about looking like a beginner, I get it. I'd just say: being a beginner is the good part.

## Beginning again

There's a joy in discovery that I'd forgotten about. The feeling of being a kid again, figuring something out for the first time, and wanting to show someone.

That's what this blog is for. Not because I have it all figured out, but because I'm starting over, and I expect I'll keep starting over. Again, and again, and again.

More soon.
